Tips to Take Care: Everyday Lung & Breathing Support

Evidence-based breathing exercises and protective self-care practices

Pursed-Lip Breathing Technique

How to do it: Inhale slowly through your nose for 2 seconds. Pucker your lips like blowing out birthday candles, and exhale gently for 4 full seconds.

Benefit: Keeps airways open longer during exhalation, clearing trapped stale air and calming shortness of breath.

Diaphragmatic (Belly) Breathing

How to do it: Sit comfortably with one hand on your belly. Inhale through your nose, feeling your belly push your hand outward. Exhale gently while belly falls inward.

Benefit: Strengthens the main breathing muscle (diaphragm) and relieves tension in neck and shoulder muscles.

Hydration & Mucus Clearance

Drink 6–8 glasses of warm water or herbal tea daily. Staying hydrated keeps airway secretions thin and much easier to clear naturally without intense coughing.

Tip: Avoid ice-cold beverages if you notice they trigger bronchial spasms or cough.

Avoid Respiratory Triggers

Stay away from secondhand tobacco smoke, wood-burning fireplaces, harsh cleaning chemicals, and aerosol sprays. Use a HEPA air purifier in the bedroom.

Cold Weather: Wrap a light scarf over your mouth and nose when going outside in cold, dry weather.

Gentle Daily Walking

Aim for 15–20 minutes of leisurely walking on level ground each day. Pace your steps with your breathing (e.g., inhale for 2 steps, exhale for 4 steps).

Pacing: Stop and rest whenever needed; regular gentle activity expands functional lung capacity over time.

When to Seek Immediate Medical Help

  • Sudden severe shortness of breath that does not ease after rest.
  • Bluish tint around your lips, tongue, or fingernails.
  • Chest tightness, pressure, or sharp pain when inhaling.
  • High fever with persistent coughing up of thick yellow/green mucus.

Tips to Take Care: Everyday Vocal & Motor Wellness

Evidence-based vocal strengthening drills, fine-motor exercises, and safety tips

Daily Sustained "Ahhh" Loudness Drills

How to do it: Take a deep breath and vocalize a loud, clear "aaah" at a comfortable pitch, holding it as steady as you can for 10–15 seconds. Repeat 5 times daily.

Benefit: Strengthens vocal cord closure, prevents speech fading, and improves breath support coordination.

Pitch Glides & Expressive Reading

How to do it: Read a book or newspaper paragraph aloud for 5 minutes using exaggerated pitch changes and enthusiastic vocal inflection.

Benefit: Counters monotone vocal pitch (hypophonia) and keeps laryngeal muscles flexible.

Finger Tapping & Hand Opening Sequences

How to do it: Open and close your fingers as wide and fast as possible for 10 reps. Then tap your thumb to each fingertip one by one, forward and backward.

Benefit: Stimulates fine motor coordination in the fingers and combats morning stiffness.

Big & Bold Handwriting Practice

Spend 5 minutes each day writing sentences on lined paper using deliberately large, tall cursive letters.

Benefit: Re-trains brain motor circuits to prevent handwriting shrinkage (micrographia).

Posture & Fall Prevention

Walk with deliberate heel-to-toe strides with normal reciprocal arm swing. Keep hallways well-lit and remove loose throw rugs or cords.

Tip: Avoid sudden pivoting turns; take a gentle U-turn curve with small steps when turning around.

When to Consult a Neurologist

  • A resting tremor in your fingers, hand, or chin that occurs when muscles are relaxed.
  • Stiffness or heaviness in arms or legs when walking or rolling over in bed.
  • Noticeable slowness when standing up from a low sofa or buttoning clothing.
  • Noticeable softening of your voice that others repeatedly mention.
